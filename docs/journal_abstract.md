> The integration of Large Language Models into personal finance applications introduces critical data privacy risks, including the exposure of Personally Identifiable Information to third-party LLM APIs and susceptibility to prompt injection via financial data pipelines [5]. While existing literature identifies these as significant threats to LLM-integrated systems [2][3][6][7], few studies propose deployable mitigation architectures evaluated against fintech-specific attack vectors or compared against established open-source guardrail tools.
> 
> 
> This paper makes three contributions. First, it proposes a taxonomy of eight prompt injection vectors specific to personal finance applications, including transaction description injection, bank statement import injection, obfuscated encoding attacks — including homoglyph substitution, morse encoding, and leetspeak variants — and fabricated context manipulation, vectors underrepresented in general-purpose injection corpora [3][6]. Second, it presents an eight-layer middleware guardrail architecture designed to intercept, sanitise, and validate data flows between a personal finance application and an external LLM API. The layers comprise an obfuscation-resistant input sanitiser, a structural separator for instruction boundary enforcement [6], a PII redaction layer employing Named Entity Recognition with UK financial entity recognisers [8][10], an output validator preventing action hijacking and response-borne exfiltration [4], a context provenance tracker for indirect injection detection in imported financial data, a continuous risk-scoring policy engine computing a composite threat score across five dimensions, a declarative action allowlist enforcing separation between LLM proposal and system execution, and a canary token system detecting prompt extraction and context leakage. The system aligns with GDPR Article 25 principles of privacy by design [1]. Third, it introduces an adaptive red-team evaluator that generates mutation variants of known attacks to measure robustness beyond static benchmarks.
> 
> Evaluation against a purpose-built corpus of 107 synthetic attack cases across eight fintech-specific vectors demonstrates a 100% block rate on the internal corpus with 0% false positives across 60 legitimate financial queries. Extended adaptive evaluation across 377 cases — including 270 mutation variants generated by five evasion strategies — yields an overall detection rate of 57.1%, rising to 100% against direct instruction override attacks. Comparison against four baselines — Presidio, LLM Guard [9], deepset DeBERTa, and PromptGuard 86M — demonstrates that general-purpose classifiers are poorly suited to fintech deployment: PromptGuard 86M produces an 80.4% false positive rate on legitimate financial queries, while our system maintains 0% FPR at 40 times lower mean latency than LLM Guard (5.8ms vs 229.9ms). Semantic preservation evaluated via ROUGE [11] and BERTScore yields mean ROUGE-1 of 0.986 and BERTScore F1 of 0.772, indicating acceptable utility preservation after PII redaction.
> 
> This work contributes a replicable, open-source middleware architecture for privacy-preserving and injection-resistant LLM integration in fintech systems, offering a practical engineering pathway toward GDPR-compliant [1] and trustworthy AI deployment in financial services [5].
